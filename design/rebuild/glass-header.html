A Liquid Glass header

Every phone below is live, so you can scroll inside it. Each one opens mid-scroll, with the header back over the content, which is the case that broke in your recording. Scroll down to send the header away, and scroll up to bring it back. At the very top of a page there's no glass: the page is one surface, as it is today.

About the "old frost" in your recording: no frost code is left in the app. I checked the shipped code, and there's no frost or material anywhere. The grey band you saw is iOS's own edge blur, which is lighter than it looked in my mockup, and it disappeared when the header came back mid-scroll. Both options below replace it with real Liquid Glass.

One note: iOS 26 doesn't put glass behind a whole header by default. Apple's own apps only put glass on the buttons. So this is Apple's glass material (`glassEffect`), set behind our header. In dark mode it darkens what's under it rather than lightening it, which is the opposite of the old frost.

Screen

1. Full-width glass barRecommended

One sheet of glass from the top of the screen to just under the header, edge to edge, with a thin bright rim along its bottom. The buttons sit on it as plain icons, because glass on glass looks muddy. It's the same on every screen: Journal, Feed, Saved, You and every pushed page.

2. Floating glass panel

The same glass as a rounded panel inset 8pt from the edges, under the clock, like Safari's compact bar. It feels lighter, but it frames less of the screen, leaves a sliver of content showing at each side, and needs the page colour behind the clock whenever content is under it.
