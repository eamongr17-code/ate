# Ate — brand strategy

Written by Eamon (CEO, brand owner), 2026-09-21. Verbatim. The design in `docs/DESIGN.md` is the
ratified interpretation of it. Nobody edits this file but him.

## Manifesto

Food is the unassuming witness to some of our greatest memories. Whether its a first date, a catchup
with friends, a birthday celebration or a drunken night out on the town - food is always there.

Great food, just like an iconic song or a beautiful sunset, is a sensory experience that can be
neurologically linked back to some of our favourite memories in life.

Our taste in food, just like our lives, is unique. It's crafted and controlled by us, every dish
builds a picture of who we are. Our opinions on food are shaped by our culture, our family, friends
and lifestyle. As the saying goes, you are what you eat.

So, just like a great playlist, or a photo album of a holiday - there deserves to be a place to store
and save all of our favourite food memories. A place to celebrate and share the amazing food that has
shaped our lives.

*Ate* exists for this one purpose: **To Share the Love of Food.**

## Target audience — New Age Foodies

They are the person their friends text when looking for somewhere to eat. They already have three
tabs open. They don't just go to restaurants, they research them. They photograph their food not for
content, but because a great dish deserves to be remembered. They have opinions, they have
favourites, and they are quietly building a taste identity that means something to them, with nowhere
real to show it.

They anchor their social calendar around food and plan their travel itineraries the same way. They
have disposable income and spend it on dining without apology. They follow food people online, notice
when a restaurant changes its menu, and genuinely mourn when a great place closes.

They love food. And they love sharing.

## Mission, vision

**Mission** — To Share the Love of Food.

**Vision** — A world where what you ate is a record worth keeping. The collective taste of the people
who genuinely love food becomes the most powerful force in food discovery, reshaping how restaurants
are found, chosen, and booked. A dish-level taste graph built by real foodies disrupts restaurant
tech from the ground up, making the people who care most about food the ones who shape how everyone eats.

## Positioning pillars

**Your Taste** — Ate is built around you. Your dishes, your ratings, your profile. Each person's
taste is completely their own, and over time your Ate becomes a genuine record of who you are as an
eater. The visual identity follows the same logic: personal, distinct, worth showing off.

**Bring Receipts** — Food memories deserve evidence. Ate's visual language is drawn from the receipt:
the original food memory, printed, pocketed, and eventually forgotten. We take that humble artefact
and make it worth keeping. Receipt-native design: the typography, the structure, the feel of every log.

**Ate That** — The name holds two meanings at once. Past tense of eat: a record of what you ate. And
the slang: you crushed it. The brand lives in that space. Direct, playful, and self-assured across
every touchpoint. Fluent in food culture, never precious about it.

## Creative direction — Made to Order

Ate is all about the love of food, so our creative direction borrows from materials, textures and
elements that are restaurant-native. Ornate cloth patterns, sauce-made fonts and receipt design
language that influences our App UI. It plays in the tension between the mess of food and the
structure of receipts.
